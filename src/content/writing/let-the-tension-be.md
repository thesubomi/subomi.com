I'm writing this on a train to Kraków. I haven't been to my favourite city in Poland in a while, and it was time to fix that. If you ever get the chance to visit this beautiful country, tell the pilot to drop you off in Kraków first. Thank me later.

Somewhere along the way, I started going through the pile of screenshots I'd collected while scrolling LinkedIn this week. This is the one I chose to write about, because when I first saw it, I felt seen.

## The tension I've been carrying

For the longest time I've been battling with a question about how to lead. Do I show up as the stern, slightly cold leader who keeps everyone on their toes? Or the warm, cheerful one people actually enjoy being around?

Here's the honest part: too much of the warm and cheerful doesn't hold up over time. Things slip. Lines blur. You do need to find a balance.

What this post did was put words to that balance. Being able to lock in and get things done, then switch a few minutes later and be the goofball in the room, is a rare thing. Reading that made sense of the tension I've been grappling with. It isn't a flaw I need to fix. It's the point.

## What I'm doing differently

From now on, I'm going to express myself more freely with my team. When it's time to work, we work. When it's time to play, we do exactly that.

I've always been a chief campaigner of "work hard, play harder." Bringing it to my team simply extends that thinking to the people I spend most of my time with.

## Let it

My wife has been playing with Mel Robbins' "Let Them" idea lately, and she can't stop raving about it. My version is shorter: let it. Let the tension be.

It's normal, and you should welcome it. It's part of what makes me unique, and you too, if you feel the same way.
